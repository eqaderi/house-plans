# Research: everyday usability (2026-10-13)

What published sources say about the owner's "Everyday use" scenarios in
`docs/brief.md`. Each item gives the guidance, the number if the source has
one, the source's own words, the URL and a tag:

- **[primary]**: the standard, regulation or book itself, read by us.
- **[secondary]**: a reliable summary or a study whose full text we did not read.
- **[weak]**: a search-engine snippet or interpretive source, not verified.

**Status of the sources.** None of these bind this site. The owner has said
no rules apply (brief, "Site and legal"). The UK documents are cited as
evidence of good practice, not as law:

- Lifetime Homes was replaced in 2015 by Approved Document M, category M4(2).
- The London Housing Design Guide (2010) was an interim edition, since superseded.
- The Housing Quality Indicators (HQI, v2, 2000) are withdrawn. The URL below is a mirror.
- The Pattern Language text is an unofficial online transcription of the
  book (Alexander et al., 1977).

**Mapping "entrance storey" to this house.** The road level holds only the
garage, so it contains no habitable room. AD M M4(1)(d) covers that case:
"Visitors can access and use the habitable rooms and a WC within the entrance
storey of the dwelling (or the principal storey where the entrance storey does
not contain a habitable room)". [primary] So every rule below about the
"entrance storey" or "entrance level" applies here to the **living floor**
(living, kitchen, dining, WC, prayer room). The garage level is the arrival
point, linked to the living floor by the lift and the stair.

## Checks we can compute from a plan

Each check says whether a source gives a threshold. Where none does, the
check is **"owner's criterion, compare options"**: compute it for every option
and rank them; there is no pass or fail.

| # | Check | How to measure it on the plan | Threshold | Source |
