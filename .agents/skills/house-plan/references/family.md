# A family house in Iran: what makes it work

Distilled from owner-regret surveys (roomsketcher.com/?p=11297,
builderonline.com "10 design regrets"), Christopher Alexander's *A Pattern
Language* (APL), aging-in-place standards (NAHB CAPS, Houston 2026) and
Persian sources on Iranian house planning (virgool.io, ensani.ir 406881,
wikifeqh.ir). **[C]** = computable from the spec; **[J]** = judgement;
**(U)** = weak or unverified source. Apply in priority order; P1 items are the
ones families regret most.

## P1. Zoning, privacy, circulation

1. **Public → private gradient** (APL 127 Intimacy Gradient): entry → guest
   reception → family living and kitchen → bedrooms. [C] No route from the
   entrance to a bedroom passes through the reception room; no guest route
   enters the bedroom zone.
2. **No bedroom door straight off living or reception**; give bedrooms a hall
   or vestibule. [C]
3. **محرمیت — no view from the open front door** to a WC or bath door, a
   bedroom door, or the cooking area. Traditionally a هشتی (vestibule). [C]
   sightline test from the entrance opening.
4. **An entry zone between outside and living**, never a front door straight
   into the living room. [C]
5. **Guest WC near the entry**, reached without crossing the bedroom zone,
   kitchen or laundry, and not opening off the reception room. [C]
6. **Bedrooms quiet**: no shared wall with kitchen, living, laundry or the
   pump room; wardrobes between rooms as acoustic buffers. [C]
7. **Short passages**; long narrow corridors are wasted area. [C]
   (`CIRCULATION_SHARE`, `CIRCULATION_DETOUR`)
8. **Doors are the most common builder error**: swings into appliances,
   cabinets, other doors, or onto the bed wall. [C] (swing checks)

## P2. Iranian specifics

9. **پذیرایی separate from نشیمن**: the guest room by the entry, the family
   room by the kitchen — today's form of بیرونی / اندرونی. [C]
10. **Kitchen out of guests' view and smell-contained**: Iranian cooking is
    heavy on frying and spice. A door or sliding screen to the reception, a
    ducted hood to outside, a window. Open or closed is the family's choice
    [J]; the window and the sightline are [C].
11. **Qibla**: a toilet's axis neither faces nor backs the qibla (fiqh, not
    the building code). Compute the great-circle bearing from the site to
    Mecca; flag WC axes within about ±20° (U) of it or its reverse. [C] once
    fixtures are modelled.
12. **Prayer place**: a quiet clean spot of about 1 × 2 m with a free wall
    toward the qibla, near water for wudu (U). [C]/[J]
13. **Toilets**: at least one western (فرنگی) toilet for elders and bad
    knees; often also an Iranian one. Hand sprayer and floor drain in every
    WC; WC separate from the shower room (U). [J]
14. **Gatherings** (Nowruz, Yalda, mourning): reception and dining open into
    one space for a سفره of 20–30 people, with storage for cushions and
    folding chairs (size U). [C] target area from the brief.
15. **Courtyard (حیاط) as an outdoor room**, private from neighbours'
    windows; a usable roof (مهتابی) needs a safe stair and a parapet. [J]
16. **انباری**: cool, dark, ventilated bulk-food store near the kitchen or
    service door (APL 145; size U). [C]
17. **Water tank, pump and boiler room (موتورخانه)**: water pressure is
    unreliable; roughly 100–200 L storage per unit (U). Away from bedroom
    walls, with maintenance access. [C]
18. **کفش‌کن**: shoe storage for family and guests and a bench inside the
    entrance; about 1.5 m² of storage zone (U). [C]

## P3. Rooms

19. **Entry**: coats and shoes face the door. Nobody regrets a bigger one.
20. **Kitchen**: work triangle legs 1.2–2.7 m, total 4.0–7.9 m, no tall unit
    across a leg; aisle ≥ about 1.07 m (1.2 m for two cooks); landing counter
    beside stove, sink and fridge; a window over the sink (APL 192). [C] once
    fixtures are modelled.
21. **Dining next to the kitchen** (a top regret when far). [C]
22. **Laundry** near the bedrooms or on a service balcony (بالکن خدماتی)
    with a drain and outdoor drying. [C]
23. **Master suite**: vestibule, wardrobe ≥ 60 cm deep, own bath. [C]
24. **Children**: their realm near family living (APL 137), a place of their
    own each. [J]
25. **A ground-floor bedroom with a full bath** for guests, elders, and
    growing old in the house. [C]
26. **Storage**: "too little storage" is the most-named annoyance (45 %).
    "10 % of floor area" is one blog's figure (U); the UK space standard sets
    per-bedroom minimums instead. [C] against the brief's target.

## P4. Furniture first

27. **Test-fit furniture before approving a room.** A bedroom needs one
    unbroken wall of bed width + 2 × 60 cm (no door, window or radiator) with
    about 60 cm clear on three sides (U). The TV wall is not opposite a window
    (glare, 22 %). No columns in the reception. [C] once furniture is modelled.
28. **Outlets from the furniture plan** — the #1 regret (56 %). Final stage.

## P5. Daylight and orientation

29. **Light on two sides of every main room** (APL 159). [C] count exterior
    walls with windows per room.
30. **Living to the south, bedrooms east (APL 138), service north or west**,
    adapted to climate (cold mountain zone: south glazing for winter sun,
    small north and west openings). [C] given the north arrow.

## P6. Future-proofing

31. **Step-free and wide**: door leaves ≥ 90 cm (≈ 80 cm clear), thresholds
    ≤ 13 mm, one step-free entrance, a 150 cm turning circle in at least one
    bathroom, no level changes within a floor. [C]
32. **Plan for change**: children growing up, elderly parents moving in
    (common in Iran); don't oversize. [J]

## Red flags to reject a layout on sight

A front door opening onto the sofa; a WC door facing the living room or the
entrance; a bedroom reached through the living room; a bath reached through a
bedroom (unless en-suite); a kitchen nobody can see out of; a corridor longer
than the room it serves; a bedroom with no wall for the bed; a reception room
the guests cross the family's space to reach.
