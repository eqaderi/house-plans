# Owner design rules for my-house (binding for every agent)

These are the homeowner's rules for designing this house. Every AI agent must follow them: Claude Code, Codex, Gemini or any other tool.

- **Read this file in full before you draw, revise, critique or present any layout.**
- Every rule is a hard check. A design that breaks a rule must not be shown to the owner, and must not be presented as an option or as a compromise.
- If a rule truly cannot be met on this site, stop. Before drawing anything, explain why to the owner with a section or numbers, and ask.

The owner's exact words are in the project record (`house-ai statement list projects/my-house`) and in `design_brief.yaml`. This file is an ordered summary of them. Where the two seem to differ, the verbatim words win, so quote them and ask the owner.

Sources are cited as `stmt-NNN` (owner statement) or `brief` (interview answer in `design_brief.yaml`).

## 1. Kitchen

1. **The kitchen opens directly onto a terrace on the south side, at least, and looks south.**
   - A door from the kitchen goes straight out onto that terrace.
   - A kitchen facing the west court, or one that reaches the terrace only through another room, fails.
   - The owner first said this on 2026-10-21 (stmt-012: *"the kitchen must definitely have a scenery towards south at least, and it should be connected to a terrace. This is not acceptable. It should have a terrace."*). Every option in C04 and C05 still broke it, and the owner rejected all of them for it (stmt-017).
2. **The kitchen gets prime frontage, south and/or east.** It is never the room squeezed into whatever is left after the core and the other rooms are placed. (stmt-012, stmt-017)
3. **Semi-open kitchen, with cooking smell kept out of the living areas.**
   - Preferably no doors, unless one is truly needed.
   - Either enclosed with two openings or walk-throughs at two ends or sides, or open with walls reaching the ceiling. (brief, interview rounds 5 and 7)
4. Everyday meals are eaten in the kitchen. The dining area sits next to the kitchen, for guests. (brief) The dining may open fully into the living room as one L-shaped living-dining room (stmt-024).
5. **Kitchen and dining must be right-sized, never bloated at the expense of the living room.** Keep the Kitchen around `15–17 m²` (plus a `4–5 m²` Pantry) and the Dining area around `13.5–15.5 m²` (`>= 3.2 m` wide for 6–8 seats) so that the East Family Living Room is generous and wide. (stmt-021: *"Kitchen and dining are too big and living room is too small in all of them."*)

## 2. Circulation: how you reach rooms

1. **Every main room is reached directly from the entrance or stair hall, never through another main room.**
   - You must never walk through the kitchen and then the dining room to reach the living room. (stmt-017: *"you have to go to the kitchen and then to the dining and then reach the living room … it's absurd"*)
   - Check: for each of living, kitchen, dining and every bedroom, a route from the hall exists that crosses no other main room.
2. **No long hallways, and no "prison, hospital, school or university" layout with rooms left and right of a corridor.**
   - An entrance hallway is fine. Open areas are better. (brief, interview round 23; stmt-012 *"little places and little hallways"*)
3. **No voids, leftover holes or "puzzle" layouts.** Every square metre belongs to a usable room. (stmt-012)
4. **No unnecessary doors or barriers.** (stmt-014)
5. **Never walk through a closet (`DRESS`) to reach the parents' bedroom.** The bedroom itself must open directly from the hall or family arrival; the walk-in closet (`DRESS`) and private ensuite open from inside the bedroom. (stmt-021: *"To go to the parent's bedroom, you have to go through the closet sometimes."*)
6. **The front / arrival door must be clear and direct, never routed through strange passages, doglegs, or notches behind stores.** Both the Road Level pedestrian front door and the Main Floor west court arrival door must sit directly on the West arrival façade and lead straight into a clean rectangular entrance hall toward the stair/lift core. (stmt-021: *"Also, the door for the front is not clear sometimes. It goes through strange passages."*)

## 3. Living room

1. **The living room needs a cozy, sheltered sitting corner.**
   - The owner pictured an L-shaped living room with its northern side closed off.
   - It must not be a long walk-through strip where people look straight into the kitchen and dining room, or straight onto the stair hall. (stmt-015)
2. **Closed off does not mean cut off.**
   - The living room still has its own direct entry from the hall, as rule 2.1 requires.
   - C05 obeyed 3.1 by removing every opening to the hall, and so broke 2.1. Never trade one rule for the other.
3. **The living room must be wide (`>= 5.0 m` across its main sitting zone, about `34 m²`, e.g. 5 × 6.8 m), never squeezed into a narrow `2.2–3.8 m` strip.** Superseded size: `>= 42 m²` (stmt-021) was relaxed by the owner to ~34 m² so the house fits the field (stmt-024). (stmt-021: *"Kitchen and dining are too big and living room is too small in all of them."*)

## 4. Site use and massing

1. **South and east are the most precious frontage.**
   - Use them for the day rooms (kitchen, living, dining) and terraces, and use the sun from south and east wisely.
   - Wasting them, for example with stores or a stair on the best façade, fails. (stmt-012)
2. **The house must not be narrow towards the east.**
   - The lower floors step further east, following the slope. This also keeps excavation down. (stmt-012: *"as we go down, they should go more towards east"*; stmt-017: *"I don't know why these houses are narrow towards east"*)
   - **Confirmed in stmt-018:** Step the 3 levels further east to `u ≈ 20 m` (e.g. Road `1–11 m`, Main `2–16 m`, Lower `6–20 m` + terrace), giving deeper east–west floors, big terraces, balanced digging, and a ~10 m flat east lawn.
3. **Plenty of terraces on the main floor (below the garage), in every direction except west.**
   - Most of them sit on the roof of the floor below. (stmt-012)
   - There is a balcony on the upper floor. It can be the roof of the floor below, or it can stand free and shade the floor below. (brief, interview round 30)
4. **There is no fixed digging limit, but digging must stay reasonable.** Both a 9 m dig and a house sitting fully above the soil were rejected. (stmt-013)
5. Show a section of the field, its slope and the excavation with every design. (stmt-012)
6. A big garden is wanted, but not at the cost of rules 1 to 4. (stmt-012)
7. **The house must fit the 600 m² field comfortably and leave a real garden on the east, down the hill.** Nothing (house, terraces, paths) may cross the boundary or the 1 m setbacks. A house of about 525–555 m² gross, covering about half the field, with only a 35–43 m² lawn, is too big and fails. **Target (stmt-024): lower floor ends at u ≈ 18 m, leaving ~150 m² of east garden; excavation stays ≤ ~4.5 m; keep a separate downstairs lounge.** (stmt-023: *"It is too big. It's basically going outside the boundaries of our field... There is no place for a garden on the eastern side. Down the hill"*)

## 5. Garage, lift and stair

1. **There is an indoor route from the garage to the lift, for carrying groceries.** Having to walk outside from the garage to reach the lift fails. (stmt-015)
2. **One stair connects the road level, the main floor and the lower floor, and works without electricity.** The owner wants the flights connected almost seamlessly. (stmt-015)
3. **The stair is part of the household, at the heart of the house.** It must not be hidden in a corner. (stmt-015)
4. Older parents, or anyone with a broken leg, must be able to avoid stairs, so they need a lift and step-free routes. (brief, interview round 19)
5. The garage holds 2 cars comfortably and can be closed; it is not an open carport. It is at road level, and the house steps down below it. **Confirmed in stmt-018:** Use an oversized garage (~55–60 m²) with an integrated workbench/workshop bay along one side, plus an indoor door straight to the central Lift + Stair Hall, and the Quiet Office + WC/shower looking east onto the roof deck. (brief, stmt-018)

## 6. Rooms

1. The master bedroom has its own bathroom; the other bedrooms share bathrooms. Ideally each floor has one bathroom. (brief)
2. The children share rooms, two per room. (brief)
3. The workshop is integrated into the oversized garage (~55–60 m²) rather than a narrow sliver, and there are no stores in odd places (tuck the lower store next to the laundry/core). (stmt-015, stmt-018)
4. Bedrooms are kept away from the living areas, while the house still feels open. (brief, interview round 31)
5. Keep 1 Grand Master Suite on the Main Floor (tucked on the quiet north side) + 3 bedrooms on the Lower Floor, with Central Hall → direct entry to South Kitchen, Dining, and SE/East L-shaped Living. (stmt-018)
6. Include a compact tea/snack bar (sink, mini-fridge, kettle counter) in the downstairs Family Room so no one has to climb stairs for water/tea/snacks at night. (stmt-015, stmt-018)
7. **Heavily base the floor-plan geometry and spatial organization on the owner's crafted designs (`user-designed/redesign-0109-r5` Options A, B, C) while resolving all defects in `independent-review-0110-r2` (stmt-019, stmt-020):**
   - **Do NOT reuse `argon-c06`'s rectangular floor plates with cosmetic labels added (rejected in `stmt-020`).** The actual room arrangements, stepped/notched footprints, north-projecting vertical core (`CORE 24R` + `REST 1.40` + `LIFT`), and 4-part screened wet/wudu cluster must come directly from the owner's `0109-r5` proposals (`A`, `B`, `C`), upgraded to satisfy every rule in this file and fix `0110-r2`.
   - **Road Level (`0109-r5` West-to-East sequence):** Two full-size independent car bays on the west (`two 3.35 m` west shutters, LC300 + second car, `0.65 m` door-opening zones with `0` clash, fixing I09) → central **Six-Person Unloading** zone (`6 lockers` on north wall + **Lift** at north) → east side with the **North-Projecting Core Stair (`REST 1.40`)**, **Replaceable Plant** (`1.20 m` west replacement door into Unloading), and **Quiet Office / Gaming** (+ WC) at the SE corner.
   - **Main / Upper Floor:** Follow the owner's `0109-r5` spatial arrangements (e.g., Option B's NW **Walk-in Pantry** + SW/W **Parents Suite** [`Bedroom + Private Dress + Private Accessible Ensuite`] + North-Center **4-part Screened Wet & Wudu Cluster** [`Shared WC + Shared Shower` at north, `Wudu + Screened Multiuse Lobby` at south with opaque baffle] + **South Kitchen** (`~18–22 m²`, fixing I26) opening to the **South Terrace** + **SE Dining (6–8)** + **East Family Living & NE Reading/Prayer Bay** with a direct short doorway from Wudu, fixing I11; or Option A/C's compact/staggered upper day bar with West Pantry/Suite, South Kitchen, South-Center/SE Dining, and East Living/Prayer), ensuring **Central Hall / Family Link** gives direct access to Kitchen, Dining, and Living without walking through another main room (Rule 2.1).
   - **Lower Floor:** Follow the owner's `0109-r5` lower-floor arrangements: **Boys** (`4.3×4.8 m`, SW) and **Girls** (`4.3×4.8 m`, South-Center) side-by-side along the south wall (`B1, B2, D1, D2, W1, W2` in each), **Grandparents / Guest Suite** (SE in Option B, with its own **private accessible seated WC + shower en-suite**, fixing I10) or **NE Parents Suite** (`Parents + Private Ensuite + Private Dress` at NE in Options A/C), **Garden Lounge + Tea/Snack Bar** on the east opening to the East Garden, and **Laundry / Linen / Drying** (`~12–14 m²`, fixing I12) + **Household Store** (fixing I22) tucked beside the Core Stair.

## 7. Drawings shown to the owner

1. Room labels never overlap and are readable at a glance. (stmt-015)
2. Every design shows all levels: road level with the garage, main floor, lower floor and garden (stmt-016), plus the section required by rule 4.5.
3. Show furnished room layouts (2 cars + door swing zones, 6 lockers, workbench, kitchen counters, 6–8 seat dining, L-sofa + cozy/prayer nook, master bed/dress, twin beds/desks/wardrobes in children's rooms, WC sightline baffles, exterior garden stair) clearly without label collisions. (stmt-019)
4. **Design like a seasoned architect, never "robotic".** Every room has a reason to be where it is, real walls, doors and furniture, and a size that is standard and comfortable for a family: not oversized, not cramped. Rectangles packed into a grid with no thought behind them fail. (stmt-022: *"They just seem very robotic... unfinished, childish"*; *"every room, every area should be standard. We don't want big unnecessarily. We don't want small"*)

## 8. Process (every agent, every time)

1. **Check every rule above before presenting.** Put a short checklist of rule number → pass, with the evidence, next to every design you deliver.
2. **When the owner asks for options, give at least three complete designs that are genuinely different and that all make sense.** (stmt-015, stmt-016)
3. **Do not generate designs the owner did not ask for.** The owner's token budget is limited. (stmt-017)
4. **Record new owner feedback verbatim** (`house-ai statement add … --actor agent:<tool>`), then add it to this file as numbered rules.
5. **Every delivery of two or more options includes a comparison view at the top of its page.** The owner must see at a glance how the options differ (stmt-025):
   - a table that lists **only the items that differ** between options (room positions and sizes, what sits in each corner, terraces, total floor area, ground covered, garden, earthwork, open decisions), with the identical items folded away;
   - the **same floor of every option side by side**, cropped to the house, with each differing room or terrace **outlined in orange**, and a caption under each image naming those rooms;
   - one tab per floor (road, main, lower) plus the sections.

   The reference implementation is `design/claude-c09/compare.py` (`section()` builds the HTML, and `render()` draws the outlined plans). Reuse it, or build the same view for your own output. Plain separate pages per option fail this rule.
