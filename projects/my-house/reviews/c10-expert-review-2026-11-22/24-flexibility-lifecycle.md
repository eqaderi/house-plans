# 24 · Flexibility, adaptability and lifecycle (30+ years) · C10

Concept stage; nothing engineered or code-checked. Household (brief rounds 1, 19): couple, 2 sons (~10), 2 daughters (unborn), possibly grandparents permanently. Peak demand in 8–18 years.

## Summary

1. C10 suits today's household, but its rooms are fixed for life. Neither 4.3 × 4.8 m children's room can split, and no room can become a bedroom without breaking rule 2.1.
2. The brief's verbatim "lower floor should be accessible from outside too" is not met: the lower floor has no entrance of its own and cannot be closed off as a separate unit.
3. Ageing owners can almost live on the main floor alone. The ensuite is the problem: the only way in is through the dressing room and two 0.8 m doors. The laundry is also two floors down.
4. The lift shaft (2.0 × 2.3 m) is too short for a stretcher car, and no pit, overrun or replacement route is reserved.
5. Nothing says which walls carry load, and walls on different floors don't line up. Built as bearing walls, they would freeze the lower floor.

## What C10 gets right

- The lift and the stair serve all three floors. Road level is step-free from the forecourt (1677.40 → 1677.50).
- The main floor holds master + ensuite, kitchen, living, dining and level terraces: the right floor for old age.
- The NE grandparents' suite has a seated shower and a step-free garden door, 2 m from the lift. With the lounge and tea bar beside it, it is a ready-made future flat.
- The office is reached straight from the front door and has its own WC and shower, which suits work from home and visitors.
- The children's rooms hold two beds, two desks and two wardrobes each without crowding.
